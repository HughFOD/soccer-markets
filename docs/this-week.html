Does beating the consensus mean anything?

A price that beats the consensus could just be noise. The test is whether it still looks good at kick-off, when every site has caught up with the news. Free historical odds from American sportsbooks and prediction markets don't exist, so this test uses the same method on 13 European bookmakers, 2019 to 2027 (16,328 matches), comparing each flagged price with the consensus closing price.

When a price beat the consensus byPricesGap when flaggedGap at kick-offStill better at kick-off
More than 0% better2,058+0.9%+0.8%57%
More than 1% better611+2.2%+1.6%61%
More than 2% better205+3.7%+4.0%73%
More than 3% better92+5.2%+5.4%77%
More than 5% better26+8.7%+7.3%77%

Prices more than 2% better than the consensus were still better at kick-off 73% of the time, by 4.0% on average (95% range 2.8% to 5.2%). Small gaps (under 1%) are mostly noise. Profit over a few hundred bets is too noisy to judge either way, which is why the kick-off price is the better test. This is a study of pricing, not betting advice.

How it works.

Three steps, rerun every week.

Collect the odds.

From FanDuel, DraftKings, Fanatics, BetMGM and Caesars, plus Kalshi and Polymarket, through The Odds API. Caesars and Fanatics appear only on a paid data plan.

Build the consensus.

Each site's odds become chances with its cut removed, then are averaged with equal weight. No site counts more than another.

Flag the gaps.

A price more than 2% better than the consensus is flagged. Gaps over 25% are ignored as stale or mistyped prices.

Odds from The Odds API. Match history from football-data.co.uk. Prices move constantly, so check the site before acting.
