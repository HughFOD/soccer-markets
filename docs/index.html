Are the flagged bets real?

A flagged bet could just be noise. The odds just before kick-off are the most accurate there are, because by then all the news is in. So each flagged bet is checked again at kick-off: was it still a good bet? Old odds from American sportsbooks and prediction markets aren't available for free, so this test uses the same method on 13 European bookmakers, 2019 to 2027 (16,328 matches).

Flagged atBetsLooked like when flaggedWas worth at kick-offStill a good bet at kick-off
Over 0%2,058+0.9%+0.8%57%
Over 1%611+2.2%+1.6%61%
Over 2%205+3.7%+4.0%73%
Over 3%92+5.2%+5.4%77%
Over 5%26+8.7%+7.3%77%

Bets flagged at over 2% were still good bets at kick-off 73% of the time, worth 4.0% on average (95% range 2.8% to 5.2%). Gaps under 1% were mostly noise. Actual wins and losses over a few hundred bets swing too much to prove anything, so the kick-off odds are the fairer test. This is a study of pricing, not betting advice.

How it works.

Three steps, rerun every morning.

Collect the odds.

From FanDuel, DraftKings and BetMGM, plus Kalshi and Polymarket, through The Odds API.

Work out what's expected.

Each site's odds become chances with its cut removed, then are averaged with equal weight. That gives every result's expected chance.

Find the bets that beat it.

A bet makes the list when a site's odds pay more than its chance deserves. Gaps over 25% are left out as likely stale or mistyped prices.

Odds from The Odds API. Match history from football-data.co.uk. Prices move constantly, so check the site before acting.
