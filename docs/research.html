How accurate are betting odds?

52,841 matches from six top European leagues since 2000, checked against their betting odds.

See the findingsToday's best bets

What I found.

In short

  • Long shots are the worst bet. $100 on every long shot lost $27 on average; on every heavy favorite, $1. Finding 2
  • The same holds for total goals. The unlikely side of over/under 2.5 lost $14 per $100; the likely side $2. Finding 3
  • When odds move before kick-off, they're usually right. An early bet on a team whose chance then rose 5+ points made +$16 per $100. Finding 4
  • This site's method holds up. 73% of prices flagged as 2%+ better than the consensus were still better at kick-off. Finding 5
  • Teams behind at half-time score more than the odds expect: 15% more for teams losing by 2 or more. Finding 6

FINDING 1

The odds are accurate

Strong favorites that the odds gave a 67% chance of winning actually won 70% of the time. Long shots given a 12% chance won 10% of the time. So the odds are close to the truth.

They're slightly off in one consistent way: favorites win a little more often than their odds suggest, and long shots a little less. (Technically, a calibration slope of 1.10, 95% range 1.07 to 1.12, where 1.00 would be perfect.)

FINDING 3

The long-shot pattern holds for total goals too

Over/under 2.5 goals works like match results. When the odds made a side unlikely (under 35%), $100 on it lost $14 on average. When they made it likely (65% or more), it lost only $2.

FINDING 4

When odds change before kick-off, they're usually right to do so

Odds move as news comes in. The bigger the move, the more it mattered: when a team's chance rose by 5 or more percentage points before the match (say, from 40% to 45%), an early $100 bet on it made +$16. When its chance fell by 5 or more, the same bet lost $18. Betting every result at the first odds lost $4. That's why every pick on this site is checked again at kick-off.

FINDING 5

This site's flagged bets usually hold up at kick-off

A flagged bet is one where a site pays more than the bet's chance of winning deserves. Of bets flagged with a gap of 2% or more, 73% were still good bets at kick-off, when the odds are at their most accurate, worth 4.0% per bet on average. The bigger the gap, the more often it held up.

FINDING 6

After half-time, the team that's behind scores more than expected

Teams losing by 2+ at half-time scored 15% more in the second half than the odds predicted. Teams winning by 2+ scored 5% fewer.

FINDING 7

Home advantage matters

Playing at home is a real edge: in the five seasons before COVID, home teams won 45% of matches and scored +0.35 goals a game more than their opponents. When stadiums were empty (June 2020 to May 2021), that edge roughly halved: home teams won 40% and the goal margin fell to +0.17. The odds were slow to notice, so for the first months home teams were overrated.

FINDING 8

Draws are a better bet than wins at the same odds

Take every bet the odds gave a 20–35% chance. Betting $100 on the draw lost $7 on average. Betting $100 on a team to win lost $10. Both lose, because of the cut, but draws lose less.

That's because draws happen about as often as the odds say (27% expected, 27% happened), while teams given the same chance win a little less often than expected (28% expected, 27% happened).

FINDING 9

Big clubs aren't overpriced

Fans back big clubs no matter what, so you might expect their odds to be worse. At first glance it's the opposite: betting $100 on a big club to win lost $4 on average, against $8 for other teams. But big clubs are usually favorites (a 57% chance on average), and favorites lose less (Finding 2). Compare other teams at the same odds and they lost $4 too. So big clubs are priced just like everyone else.

FINDING 10

Teams with nothing to play for aren't overpriced

Late in the season, some teams can't reach Europe or promotion and can't be relegated. Against a team that still had something to play for, the team with something at stake was given a 48% chance and won 50% of the time. That gap could easily be luck, so the odds already account for motivation.

Sportsbooks against prediction markets

After their trading fees, the prediction markets take a typical cut of 6.5%, against 6.4% for the sportsbooks, so sportsbooks are cheaper today. A prediction market has the best odds on 31% of results, a sportsbook on 30% (average per site). The bigger question, whose odds are more accurate, needs a few hundred finished matches. Every morning's odds are now saved, so that test runs once there are enough.

How it's built.

  • Data. Results and odds from every bookmaker recorded for each match, from football-data.co.uk.
  • Removing the cut. Three methods compared: proportional, power and Shin's 1993 model.
  • Uncertainty. 95% ranges from resampling whole matches (the bootstrap).
  • Tests. Automated checks on every calculation.
  • Odds. Snapshots from European bookmakers collected by football-data.co.uk.
